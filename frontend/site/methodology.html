Methodology

How FactScope reaches a result—and where it stops.

FactScope organizes evidence for investigation. It does not know ground truth, replace the original source, or guarantee that every checked statement is accurate.

Analysis pipelineResult labelsConfidenceImage checksSource signalsLimitations

The analysis pipeline

A page scan passes through several distinct stages. Keeping these stages separate prevents a reputable publisher, polished writing, or a single related article from becoming proof that every claim is true.

1 · Extract

Identify checkable claims

The system looks for concrete statements about people, events, numbers, policies, dates, and places.

2 · Classify

Understand the content type

Opinion, satire, predictions, breaking news, and pages without checkable claims receive different handling.

3 · Discover

Find candidate sources

Fact-check results and news reporting are searched separately for each extracted claim.

4 · Validate

Check evidence quality

FactScope evaluates reachability, relevance, recency, independence, duplicated reporting, and self-corroboration.

5 · Present

Separate evidence from context

Direct findings, matching coverage, and broader background appear in different groups.

6 · Explain

Keep uncertainty visible

Limitations and technical failures are shown instead of being converted into a false low-trust result.

What the labels mean

LabelMeaningWhat it does not mean
SupportedQualifying direct evidence or strong independent corroboration supports the checked claim.Every detail in the page is proven.
ContradictedQualifying evidence conflicts with the checked claim.The publisher or entire page is always unreliable.
MixedEvidence is contextual, nuanced, or points in different directions.The claim is exactly half true.
Matching coverageReporting appears to discuss the same claim or event.Every detail has been directly confirmed.
Broader contextA source provides useful background but is not a close enough match.The source supports or contradicts the claim.
Still openNo qualifying external evidence was found within the scan.The claim is false.

How confidence works

Confidence describes how strongly the available evidence supports a specific FactScope finding. It is not the probability that a claim is true.

High

Direct and inspectable

Normally reserved for a reachable direct fact-check whose reviewed claim closely matches.

Medium

Strong corroboration

May come from qualifying independent reporting or an authoritative primary source.

Low / open

Not enough for a finding

Used when evidence is unavailable, indirect, still developing, or cannot be inspected strongly enough.

Coverage breadth

A different measurement

Shows how many claims have matching reports. Broad coverage can still leave exact details unresolved.

Image verification uses separate assessments

  • Visual manipulation indicators look for signs associated with generation, compositing, or editing.
  • Caption consistency compares checkable caption claims with available external reporting.
  • Visible source clues identify credits or watermarks that may help trace provenance.
Important

Visual analysis is model-based, not cryptographic or forensic verification. Editing can be legitimate, and a visible credit does not prove ownership or an unedited chain of custody.

Source and presentation signals

HTTPS, author attribution, publication date, publisher history, layout, and risky presentation patterns help users understand a page. These signals remain secondary and do not establish whether individual claims are true.

Known limitations

  • Breaking-news evidence may be incomplete or change quickly.
  • Search results can miss local, paywalled, newly published, non-English, or poorly indexed reporting.
  • Matching headlines may describe a different time, place, person, or event.
  • Syndicated reports are not necessarily independent confirmation.
  • AI models can misclassify content or overlook relevant details.
  • FactScope should not be the sole basis for medical, legal, financial, safety, or civic decisions.

For consequential claims, open the linked sources, find the primary material, compare dates and wording, and consult a qualified expert when appropriate.
